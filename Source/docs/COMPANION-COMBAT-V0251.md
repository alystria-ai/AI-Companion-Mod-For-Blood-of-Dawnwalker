# v0.25.1 — follower spacing, frame cost and campaign combat

This investigation used the running, paused game on 13 September 2026. No gameplay inputs, attacks, teleports or microphone tests were issued during the investigation. The new behaviour still needs the player's short gameplay check.

## What the evidence showed

- Every current follower had `KeepDistanceToPlayer=180` and `KeepDistanceToPlayerMoveTo=245`. Separate summon locations did not give them separate follow destinations.
- The UE4SS startup log reports that its object hash tables are unavailable. `StaticFindObject` therefore scans the object array. The party update repeatedly resolved default libraries and classes, including during navigation and recovery.
- The old catch-up code used one point 250 cm behind Coen for everyone and called `StopAllActions` after each successful teleport. The log records Bakir, Anca and Lacra catching up close together, then Anca and Lacra doing it again roughly five seconds later. Ambrus and Bakir subsequently detached from their population pawns. This is evidence of repeated recovery work; it does not by itself prove the source of every frame hitch.
- Ambrus's captured state had `weaponSelector=None`, `combatMode=None`, and no equipped selector. His loaded NPC definition contains `ITM_Weapon_SwordAmbrus1a`, a sword combat animation configuration, and supported Sword character states. His so-called Neutral state actually includes `Character.IsInCombat` and uses a combat defence movement profile.
- The live Anca and Lacra attribute sets both had `DamageAIvsAI=10`. Their maximum health values were 9,398 and 48,006. Merely inspecting a health bar could not establish which damage path was used, so the investigation followed the native attack tree and gameplay effects.
- `AssetTree_DefaultEnemy_Main` chooses between `GE_Combat_Follower_Health_Damage` and `GE_Combat_Health_DamageAIvsAI` using `BP_AssetNode_Filter_AttackerIsPlayerFollower`. That filter accepts the board tag **`RebelAI.Flag.DealFollowerDamage`** as an alternative to active follower locomotion. The AI-versus-AI effect captures **the source's** `DamageAIvsAI` with coefficient 1; the follower effect instead uses `MMC_CombatDamageFollower`. The reflected settings also expose `BaseFollowerDamage` on the player and a follower damage multiplier table. Disabling follow mode to free combat movement had not preserved this separate damage classification.
- Ambrus uses native ticket arbitration, with one standard and one helper ticket in the inspected definition, and helper cooldowns of 8–12 seconds in his config. An aggressive label in our menu previously changed enemy selection only. It did not request native aggression.

Original evidence is preserved under `runtime/session-v0251`: the pre-update UE4SS log, party snapshots, compact reflection index and 50 read-only object exports. In particular, audit 11/12/13 describes Ambrus's equipment, audit 24/34 contains the damage attributes, audit 40/41 describes his states/tickets, and audit 48 records the native follower state-tree path. `runtime/companion-native-party251damage02.txt` records the tag and both damage effects. An earlier exploratory tag read failed because the property is a tag **container**, not a single tag; the corrected probe succeeded.

## Implementation

**Spacing:** each game actor keeps its own spawn slot. Slots map to separate destinations in rows behind Coen, with 280 cm lateral and 300 cm row separation. Orientation follows travel velocity and stays fixed while Coen is stationary; rotating the camera does not rotate the formation. Navigation projects at most one formation point per party update, at most once per member per second, and only after a meaningful destination change. Projected destinations that collapse onto another reserved destination are rejected.

A scoped pre-hook on `AIController:MoveToLocation` adjusts the native follower's own move request. It runs only for a controller belonging to our current party, in travel mode, outside conversations, combat, cinematics, suspended AI and unbreakable actions. It does no searches or projection inside the hook. A stopped follower that remains outside its assigned slot can receive an occasional settle request through the same native controller. The code avoids continuously restarting movement. Hook failure disables redirection and writes a diagnostic instead of flooding errors. Cleanup unregisters both hook IDs.

Native navigation still chooses the route. Narrow paths can compress the formation; the system rejects colliding projected destinations rather than guaranteeing an impossible formation in every corridor. The native game remains responsible for collisions and local avoidance. UE's RVO and Detour systems have different component requirements; this patch does not blindly replace Dawnwalker's controller or combine avoidance systems. See [Epic's avoidance guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-avoidance-with-the-navigation-system?application_version=4.27).

**Catch-up and cost:** classes/default libraries are cached and checked for validity before reuse. Missing AI recovery backs off to eight seconds and has a party-wide polling budget. Catch-ups start earlier for ordinary parties, use separate collision-checked arrivals, wait at least eight seconds per actor and 1.5 seconds across the party, and no longer call `StopAllActions` after teleporting. A conservative rear-camera test avoids confusing shadow rendering with actual visibility. Both actor and destination must be behind the camera; combat, falling, scenes and unbreakable actions still prevent relocation. Larger formations scale the recovery distance to their slot distance. Five seconds of re-engagement delay prevents immediate return to the fight after catching up.

**Damage:** owned clones receive the verified follower-damage tag at attachment and equipment preparation. Combat may disable follower locomotion without losing the campaign follower damage classification. This is not an arbitrary damage multiplier, a direct health subtraction, or a modification to the shared gameplay effect/CDO. Boss/player encounter damage and follower damage are separate game systems; this patch aims to restore the follower system. Special attacks with their own effect paths still need observation.

**Combat:** the travel/combat controller maintains its follower-mode lease throughout combat, without restarting attacks or their introduction. It retains a valid enemy rather than oscillating between similarly close enemies. Aggressive stance now issues the native, temporary `ForceAggressionTowardsTarget` request. Authored ticket limits, costs and cooldowns remain. A start accepted by the API that stays outside native combat for 15 idle seconds receives a bounded retry; busy actions are protected. Stale preferred locations are cleared once at new encounter entry.

Sword users prepare the weapon already recorded in their own inventory through the native inventory/equipped-weapon functions. A supported sword selector, combat mode and character state are checked before handing over. This specifically covers Ambrus's missing setup, while retaining Lacra's verified claw adapter. Travel avoids a Neutral state marked as combat and uses the advertised Default state instead. The adapter neither grants powers nor rewrites shared action trees.

**Diagnostics:** each member retains twelve recent combat snapshots in `companion-history-<instance>.txt`; snapshots include physical/equipped weapons, selector, combat phase, aggression, attack ticket, selected attack, follower-damage tag and observed target health. Target health is an observation, not damage attributed to a particular companion. `companion-performance.txt` records recent mod tick timing; this is not a GPU or whole-game frame-time measurement. Snapshot work is staggered across the party.

## Limits and validation

Twenty focused companion checks and thirteen existing Lua/face/reload checks passed. All eighteen production Lua files parsed. The formation test uses forty mathematical slots, not forty in-game actors. The isolated read-only audit DLL compiled and ran without replacing the loaded population-owner DLL. No UI/helper rebuild is needed for this Lua patch.

Deployment verification: UE4SS completed the hot reload at 11:03:33 and registered the `MoveToLocation` pre/post hooks as IDs 5 and 6. The existing game process remained open and responsive. The old four-member party was released during reload. Paused, empty-party timing is recorded but is not evidence of performance while following or fighting.

Some boss abilities still assume a player target, a particular form or an encounter phase. Both inspected live combat components also expose a player-class attack filter. Its exact use varies by attack, so this patch records it rather than broadening every filter and risking unrelated targets. Restoring basic attacks, native aggression and follower damage does not prove every boss power is suitable for NPC-versus-NPC combat.

Next gameplay check: summon Ambrus with two other companions; walk and stop, run far ahead once, then observe one ordinary nearby fight. Report spacing, hitching and whether Ambrus visibly lands an attack. The automatic histories will preserve the accompanying equipment, target and combat state.

The hook and parameter handling follow [UE4SS RegisterHook](https://docs.ue4ss.com/dev/lua-api/global-functions/registerhook.html) and [RemoteUnrealParam](https://docs.ue4ss.com/dev/lua-api/classes/remoteunrealparam.html). The fixed-field diagnostic bridge uses reflection-validated property offsets and the engine's own ExportText; it never executes text returned by the game.
